## The Problem

I came into this project because members kept telling the association's staff the same thing: "I know you have this information somewhere, I just can't find it." Years of content had piled up — event recaps, policy updates, member benefits, resource libraries — all filed by whoever happened to be publishing that week, with no shared logic behind any of it.

The site wasn't badly designed. It was undesigned, in the information-architecture sense. Nobody had ever stepped back and asked what categories actually meant to a member trying to get something done.

## The Approach

I started with a full content audit — every page, cataloged by type, age, and traffic — before touching navigation or taxonomy. You can't fix structure you haven't mapped. From there I ran a handful of card-sorting sessions with actual members, not just staff, because staff already know where things are; that's exactly the blind spot.

The result was a new top-level structure organized around member tasks (renew, find an event, look up a benefit) rather than internal department names, which is how the old nav had been built. I also wrote a lightweight content style guide so future additions would actually follow the new logic instead of drifting back to ad hoc filing within a year.

## The Outcome

Support tickets asking "where do I find X" dropped noticeably in the months after launch, and staff reported spending less time manually pointing people to pages. The bigger win was durability — the new structure held up as new content types got added later, because it was built around what members were trying to do, not around org chart boxes.
