## The Problem

This client's in-house team was talented and produced good work, but every single piece of content seemed to find its own path from idea to publish. Some things went through legal review, some didn't — not because of a rule, but because whoever was managing that particular project happened to remember to loop legal in. Deadlines slipped in ways nobody could predict, because nobody had actually mapped the process being followed.

Agencies get brought in for a lot of reasons, but this one came down to: "we know we're inefficient, we just can't see why."

## The Approach

I spent time simply shadowing how content actually moved through the org — not how anyone said it worked, but what really happened from brief to publish across a handful of recent projects. That gap between the two is usually where the real problems live.

From there I built a playbook: a documented workflow with clear stage gates (brief, draft, review, legal if applicable, publish), defined owners at each stage, and templates for the recurring content types they produced most. The goal wasn't to make the process rigid — it was to make the invisible parts visible so people stopped reinventing them project by project.

## The Outcome

The team adopted the playbook faster than I expected, mostly because it matched work they were already half-doing informally — it just gave it a name and made it consistent. Turnaround time on standard content types became more predictable, and new team members had something concrete to onboard against instead of shadowing a teammate for a month to learn "how things work here."
